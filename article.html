AI agents are the new co-auditors, says our expert

Pivot insights · June 24, 2026 · 5 minute read

On this page

A new kind of team member What changes for auditors The risks to manage Getting your firm ready

A new kind of team member

Audit teams have always added new tools, from spreadsheets to sampling software. AI agents are different: they don't just calculate, they carry out multi-step tasks like tracing transactions, drafting memos and flagging anomalies across an entire ledger.

Firms piloting these agents describe them less like software and more like a junior colleague: fast, tireless and in need of supervision. That framing matters, because the profession already knows how to supervise juniors.

What changes for auditors

The obvious gain is coverage. Instead of sampling, an agent can examine every transaction and surface the exceptions worth human judgment. Routine confirmations and reconciliations shrink from days to hours.

The deeper change is where auditor time goes. Less of it is spent gathering evidence and more is spent evaluating it: challenging assumptions, weighing estimates and communicating findings to those charged with governance.

The risks to manage

Agents can be confidently wrong. They can misread context, hallucinate support for a conclusion or drift outside the scope they were given. Professional standards still place responsibility squarely on the human auditor.

That means documentation of what the agent did, review of its output with appropriate skepticism, and clear boundaries on what it is permitted to touch, especially client data and anything relevant to independence.

Getting your firm ready

Start small: one engagement, one well-defined task, one accountable reviewer. Measure the quality of the agent's work the way you would a new hire's, and expand its role only as it earns trust.

And invest in your people. The auditors who thrive alongside agents will be the ones who understand both the standards and the technology well enough to know when to overrule it.
